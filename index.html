Progression rules

Loading via RPE

  • Last working set RPE ≤ 7+2.5 kg next session (lower) / +1.25–2.5 kg (upper)
  • Last working set RPE 8Hold load, repeat next session
  • Last working set RPE 9+Drop 5%, rebuild — something's off
  • Two sessions RPE 9+ in a rowAuto-deload week, don't wait for Wk 8

Deload — Wk 8

Reduce sets by 1 across the board, drop load to 70% of recent working weights, keep swim at Tue/Thu but cut volume by 30%. Saturday and Sunday off completely. Mandatory whether or not you feel you need it — connective tissue adaptation lags muscular adaptation by weeks.

Phase 1 → Phase 2 gates (end Wk 12)

  • Swim 1 km continuousRequired
  • No knee symptoms in daily lifeRequired
  • Squat 5RM ≥ 90 kg @ RPE ≤ 8Target
  • Bench 5RM ≥ 60 kg @ RPE ≤ 8Target
  • Sleep & recovery stableRequired

Miss a "Required" gate → consolidate (repeat Wks 9–12 with technique focus). Miss "Target" only → advance to Phase 2 with lighter loading start.

Nutrition guardrails

Not a meal plan — three habits to lock in. These compound through every later phase.

  • Pre-gym breakfast (M/T/Th/F)25–40 g carb + 20–30 g protein, 60–90 min pre
  • Pre-evening-swim fuelDinner by 6 PM or "snack-dinner" at 5:30, full meal post-swim
  • Post-session within 60 min30–40 g protein + carbs
The swim digestion problem Dinner at 6:30 PM then swim at 8:15 isn't going to feel good — 90 min isn't enough for a full meal. Either eat earlier and lighter, or split dinner: small portion before, larger portion after the swim. Post-swim refeed is when most of the recovery happens anyway.
§ 04

Decision framework

If/then logic for the disruptions most likely to hit. Make the decision once, here, before you're tired and tempted to wing it.

Scenario A · Patellar tendon flare

Left knee sensitivity returns to "noticeable in daily life"

Drop squat & lunge loads 25% for 1 week. Replace Bulgarian split squats with Spanish squats & ISO holds. Do not stop training — load management beats rest. Reverse Nordics stay regardless.
Decision rule · Pain ≤3/10 during activity, gone within 24h = train modified. Pain >3/10 or lingering >24h = see physio.

Scenario B · Two weeks limited / no gym

Hotel gym only, or no gym at all

3× per week minimum, even bodyweight only. Hotel: keep 4-day structure with DB substitutes. No gym: 3× full-body bodyweight (push-up variants, single-leg squats, hinges with backpack load, pull-ups if a bar's anywhere). Swim if available. One missed week recoverable. Two weeks of nothing isn't.
Decision rule · Re-entry = drop loads 10% week 1 back, normal by week 2.

Scenario C · Strength plateau (3+ weeks no progress)

Loads stuck despite RPE ≤8

Almost always recovery or technique, rarely capacity. First: audit sleep & nutrition. Second: introduce one rep/set variation (e.g. 4×5 → 5×3 for two weeks). Third: real deload week. Only fourth: change the exercise.
Decision rule · Don't chase load before checking inputs.

Scenario D · Move abroad happens mid-phase

Relocation falls in any phase

Two-week "transition protocol": maintenance only. 3 strength (full-body), 2 swims if pool, walking for aerobic. Once new gym/pool sorted, audit equipment & revise next phase. Don't try to keep the existing programme intact through a move — that's how injuries happen.
Decision rule · Honesty about timing > pretending nothing changed.

Scenario E · Sleep <7h average for 7+ days

Work crunch, jet lag, life

Cap all sessions at RPE 7 until sleep recovers. Skip Fri finisher. Keep showing up — consistency over intensity. Don't try to "make up" sessions with weekend volume.
Decision rule · Adaptation = recovery. Insufficient recovery + same training = injury.

Scenario F · Running ramp triggers shin/calf/knee pain

Any lower limb pain on consecutive runs (Phase 2+)

One missed run = OK to push through. Two consecutive sessions with same-location pain = stop running 7 days, replace with swim or bike. Re-enter at 50% of last week's volume. Running injuries hit hardest because people ignore the first signal.
Decision rule · 10%/wk increase max. Hard ceiling 40 km/wk this year.

Scenario G · Golf wipes out Saturday

Played 18, knackered, no swim

Fine. Saturday is flex — golf priority is built in. Don't try to "make up" the missed swim midweek. The week is balanced without it.
Decision rule · If you played golf or trained, the day counted. Week doesn't need rescuing.

§ 05

Operating principles

The non-negotiables. When you forget what to do, these tell you what to do.

i.

Tendons set the ceiling

Muscle adapts in weeks, connective tissue in months. Every progression rule is paced to the slower system — your tendons. Patellar work is permanent. Tempo eccentrics in every block. You will lift less than you "could" short-term to keep lifting for the next 30 years.

ii.

Two strength sessions per week, always

No matter the phase, no matter how much running or swimming, no matter how busy. Two real strength sessions or you start losing the asset that makes everything else possible.

iii.

Mobility is non-optional

Reverse Nordic, hip flow, t-spine. These aren't finishers. They're load. The picking-the-kids-up-without-stiffness goal lives or dies on this work.

iv.

Aerobic base serves longevity first

Z2 capacity is the through-line — cardiac health, cognitive function, metabolic flexibility. The half marathon is downstream of this, not the point. Build the engine for the next 50 years, not December.

v.

Run volume has hard ceilings

10%/week increase max. 40 km/wk hard cap year one. Most adult runners get injured ignoring these — no upside in being the exception.

vi.

Mandatory deload every 4th week

Whether you feel you need it or not. The point of the deload is that you don't see the need until you've missed one and broken down. Calendared, not negotiated.

vii.

Adaptation = stimulus + recovery

Both halves matter. If sleep/stress/nutrition drop, training output drops automatically. Not weakness. How the system works. Use the decision rules.

viii.

Saturday is flex, forever

Golf is part of who you are. The plan supports the life, not the other way. Never pretend Saturday is mandatory.

What each metric tells us

Bodyweight — trend, not single readings. Drift >2 kg over 4 weeks unintended = nutrition audit.

Resting HR — earliest-warning recovery metric. RHR up 5+ bpm vs. 4-wk baseline = under-recovered. Auto-reduce intensity.

Session RPE — average across the week. Drifting up while loads stay = recovery deficit.

Sleep score — honest gut number, not a watch number. Two weeks <7 = sleep audit, cap intensity.

Knee rating — track every week even when zero. A 0→2 drift over 3 weeks is the earliest tendon overload signal — catch it here, not after the flare.
